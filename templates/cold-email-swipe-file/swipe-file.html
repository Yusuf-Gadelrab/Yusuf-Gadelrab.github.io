50 templates that get replies — not because they're clever, but because of how they're built.

Nothing here is invented "best practice." It's the shape of what actually gets a reply, with the names changed.

How to use this file

Three steps, every time. Skip step one and every template underneath reads like a mass blast — which is the one thing that kills reply rate faster than a bad subject line.

01

Research one detail

Find the single fact that proves you looked — a mantra, a recent post, a job posting, a mutual connection. See "Personalization tokens" below for exactly where to look.

02

Pick the matching template

Jump to the scenario that fits where this contact is in the relationship — first touch, third follow-up, gone quiet for two months. Wrong stage reads as tone-deaf even with perfect copy.

03

Fill the chips, cut nothing else

Every bracketed [chip] gets replaced with your real detail. The surrounding sentence structure is doing the work — resist rewriting it "to sound more like you" until you've sent it as-is at least once.

The anatomy of a cold email

One real template, annotated. Five parts, every part earning its place — nothing in a good cold email is filler.

01 · SubjectNames a specific outcome, not the product. No "Quick question" — that's a tell.
03 · ProofOne sentence, one number. Not a case study dump — just enough to be credible.
05 · SignatureReal name, real phone, one link, and the opt-out line — CAN-SPAM, and it also reads as low-pressure.
02 · OpenerThe first six words reference something true about THEM, not you. This is the whole game.
04 · AskOne CTA. Small, specific, easy to say yes to in ten seconds — never "let me know your thoughts."

1 · First-touch cold

10 templates

The very first email a contact ever sees from you. Job one is proving you're not a mass blast — job two is a single, low-friction ask.

The Mantra Mirror

01

Use when the company's own website or About page states a clear value/mission line you can quote back.

Subject
cutting [company]'s [specific pain]
Body
Hi [first name], Saw [company]'s site leads with "[their mantra/tagline]" — which usually means someone on your team is doing [specific pain] by hand to back that up. I built [one-line product description] — it's already delivered [proof point] for a [peer/industry] your size. Worth a [cta — e.g. 10-minute look]? [sender name] [phone] · [email] [city, state] Not relevant? Reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.

Why it works: quoting their own words back proves research in the first sentence, before they've decided whether to keep reading.

The Role Pain-Point

02

Use when you know the recipient's exact title and can name the tedious task that title implies.

Subject
a question about how [team/role] handles [task]
Body
Hi [first name], As [title] at [company], I'd guess a chunk of your week goes to [specific tedious task] — the kind of thing nobody was hired to do but everybody ends up doing. [product one-liner] handles that instead. No new software to learn, and it runs without [objection it removes — e.g. per-seat fees]. Open to a quick look this week? [sender name] · [phone] · [email] [city, state] — reply "no thanks" and I'll stop.

Why it works: naming the exact task their job title implies feels like insight, not a guess — even though it's often a safe bet.

The Recent-Milestone Opener

03

Use when you found a real, recent, public milestone — funding, expansion, a new hire, a press mention.

Subject
congrats on [milestone] — one thing for the growth
Body
Hi [first name], Congrats on [specific milestone, e.g. the new Austin warehouse] — that kind of growth usually means [predictable operational strain] starts showing up fast. We built [product one-liner] specifically for that stretch — [proof point] for a similar-stage team last quarter. Happy to send a 2-minute rundown if useful — no pressure either way. [sender name] · [email] · [phone]

Why it works: congratulating a real milestone is a genuine reason to reach out — it doesn't read as sales because it isn't, yet.

The Peer-Proof Opener

04

Use when you have a real result from a comparable company you can name (with permission) or describe generically.

Subject
what worked for a [peer industry] team like yours
Body
Hi [first name], A [peer company or "brokerage/agency/practice your size"] was losing [hours/dollars] a week to [specific pain] until they put [product one-liner] in front of it — [proof point] in the first month. Figured [company] might be fighting the same thing. Worth 10 minutes to see if it fits? [sender name] [phone] · [email] · [city, state]

Why it works: a named or describable peer result substitutes for trust you haven't earned yet with this specific reader.

The Job-Posting Tell

05

Use when the company is actively hiring for the exact role your product would reduce or replace the need for.

Subject
saw the [job title] posting — one alternative
Body
Hi [first name], Noticed [company] is hiring for [job title] — before that req goes further, wanted to flag an option: [product one-liner] handles a good chunk of that role's day-to-day for [price point], no ramp-up time. Not saying skip the hire — just worth seeing side by side. 10 minutes this week? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a live job posting is a public admission of the exact pain — you're not guessing, you're reading their own signal back.

The Direct-Number Opener

06

Use when you have a genuinely strong, specific number and the recipient is likely numbers-driven (ops, finance, founder).

Subject
[proof point, e.g. "60% fewer late-payment emails"]
Body
Hi [first name], Short version: [proof point in one sentence]. That's what [product one-liner] did for a team doing [what they do], similar size to [company]. If that number is interesting, I can walk you through exactly how in under 10 minutes — no deck, just the mechanics. [sender name] · [email] · [phone]

Why it works: leading with the number instead of a greeting respects a busy reader's time and filters for people who actually care about ROI.

The Curiosity-Gap Opener

07

Use when you don't have a strong personalization hook yet but want a low-risk first touch to a longer list.

Subject
the [X] hours [company] might be able to get back
Body
Hi [first name], Most teams doing [their function] are losing somewhere between [low estimate] and [high estimate] hours a week to [specific pain] without realizing it's fixable. I put together a quick way to check where [company] lands on that — takes 10 minutes, no obligation either way. Want me to send it over? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a specific range (not "a lot of time") reads as researched even when it's an industry estimate, not a company-specific one.

The Before/After Opener

08

Use when your product replaces a visibly manual process the recipient's team is known to use (e.g. spreadsheets, shared inbox).

Subject
the spreadsheet vs. the alternative
Body
Hi [first name], Most [role] teams I talk to are still running [specific manual process, e.g. rate tracking] out of a shared spreadsheet — it works until it doesn't, usually right when volume spikes. [product one-liner] replaces that with [what it does instead], [proof point]. If [company] is still spreadsheet-based, worth a look before it becomes a fire drill. 10 minutes? [sender name] · [email] · [phone]

Why it works: naming the specific tool they're probably already using (a spreadsheet) creates instant recognition without needing deep research.

The Local/Regional Opener

09

Use when you and the prospect share a city, region, or alma mater — a light, honest connection point.

Subject
fellow [city] operator — quick note
Body
Hi [first name], I'm building out of [city, state] too and came across [company] — [specific detail about them] caught my eye. I work on [product one-liner], and it fits teams doing exactly what [company] does. Would love to trade notes even if the timing's not right — open to a quick call? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a shared location lowers the guard because it reads as a real person, not a scaled campaign — use only if genuinely true.

The Blunt-Value Opener

10

Use when the recipient is senior, time-poor, and known to prefer directness over rapport-building.

Subject
[product category] for [company] — 3 lines
Body
Hi [first name], [product one-liner]. [proof point]. [price point / setup time]. If any of that lands, reply "yes" and I'll send times. If not, no worries — reply "no thanks" and you won't hear from me again. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: radical brevity signals you respect a busy inbox — and the binary ask makes replying nearly effortless either direction.

2 · Referral / warm intro

6 templates

A name to drop changes everything — these lead with the connection in the first line, where it belongs, not buried in paragraph two.

The Direct Namedrop

11

Use when a mutual contact explicitly agreed you could mention their name.

Subject
[referrer name] suggested I reach out
Body
Hi [first name], [referrer name] mentioned you're dealing with [specific pain] at [company] and thought it'd be worth a quick note — I work on [product one-liner], which is what [referrer's company, if relevant] ended up using for the same thing. Happy to do the same 10-minute walkthrough I did for them, whenever works. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: the referrer's name in the subject line alone can double open rate — it's the single strongest trust signal in cold outreach.

The CC-the-Connector

12

Use when the mutual connection is comfortable being looped in visibly, not just quoted.

Subject
intro via [referrer name] — [topic]
Body
Hi [first name] (cc [referrer name]), [referrer name] connected us — she mentioned [company] might be a fit for [product one-liner] given [specific pain referrer flagged]. I'll let you two take it from here on timing, but happy to answer anything directly. [referrer name], thanks again for the intro. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: keeping the referrer visibly on the thread adds accountability that nudges a reply, without you having to ask for one.

13

Use when you and the prospect were both at the same conference, webinar, or group, even without speaking.

Subject
fellow [event/group name] attendee
Body
Hi [first name], We were both at [event/group name] — didn't get a chance to connect there, but saw you lead [role] at [company] and wanted to follow up. I work on [product one-liner] — given [event theme/topic] was the focus, figured it might be relevant. Worth a quick chat? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a shared room is a legitimate, honest connection point that doesn't require an actual introduction to use.

14

Use when you share a school, professional group, or online community with the prospect.

Subject
fellow [school/group] alum — quick note
Body
Hi [first name], Saw we're both [school/group] — small world. I work on [product one-liner] and came across [company] while researching [their space]. Would love to trade notes even if the timing's off — but if [specific pain] is on your plate, happy to show what we built. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: an in-group signal (school, org) triggers reciprocity even between strangers — a well-documented cold-outreach lever.

The Second-Degree Ask

15

Use when you don't have permission to name-drop yet — this asks the connector to make the intro instead.

Subject
quick favor — intro to [target name]?
Body
Hi [connector name], Saw you're connected to [target name] at [company] — I think [product one-liner] could genuinely help with [specific pain] there. Any chance of a quick double-opt-in intro? Happy to send a one-line blurb you can forward, no pressure if it's not a fit for you to make. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: asking for a double opt-in intro (not a cold forward) respects the connector's relationship and gets far higher intro rates.

The Customer-Referral

16

Use when an existing happy customer offers a name in their own network.

Subject
[existing customer] thought you'd want to see this
Body
Hi [first name], I work with [existing customer] on [product one-liner] — they mentioned you might be dealing with the same [specific pain] at [company] and said it'd be worth a note. For them it meant [proof point]. Open to seeing if it maps to your setup? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a peer's satisfaction (not your pitch) is doing the persuading — customer referrals convert far above cold list outreach.

3 · Follow-up 1, 2, 3

9 templates

Three follow-ups, three different jobs. Follow-up 1 adds new information. Follow-up 2 changes the angle. Follow-up 3 makes the ask small enough that ignoring it costs more than answering it.

Follow-up 1 — Day 4, "adding one thing"

Same thread. Lead with a new, real fact — never "just bumping this up," which adds no information and reads as pressure.

The Fresh-Proof Bump

17

Use when you have a new stat, case study, or result since the first email that strengthens the case.

Subject
re: [original subject] — one more data point
Body
Hi [first name], Quick add to my last note — [new proof point since first email], which is roughly the outcome I'd expect for [company] too. Still standing by the offer: [cta]. Happy to send a couple of ideas first if that's easier than a call. Worth a look? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: new information gives the reader a legitimate reason this email exists — it's not a nudge, it's an update.

The Different-Angle Bump

18

Use when the first email led with ROI and got silence — try the time-savings or risk angle instead.

Subject
a different way to think about this
Body
Hi [first name], My last note focused on [first angle, e.g. cost] — but the bigger win for most teams is actually [second angle, e.g. getting X hours a week back]. If that's more relevant to [company] right now, happy to walk through it in 10 minutes. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: silence often means the first angle missed, not that there's no interest — a second angle costs nothing to test.

The Low-Friction Bump

19

Use when you suspect the ask (a call) was the barrier, not the offer itself.

Subject
easier version of my last ask
Body
Hi [first name], No worries if a call isn't the right first step — happy to just send a 2-minute video walkthrough of [product one-liner] instead, no meeting required. Want me to send it over? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: lowering the ask itself (not just repeating it) removes the actual objection instead of arguing around it.

Follow-up 2 — Day 9, "closing the loop"

Zero pressure, warm, and it opens the door to being redirected to the right person — often the highest-value single line in the sequence.

The Warm Close

20

Use when two touches have gone unanswered and you want one more low-key attempt before the breakup email.

Subject
closing the loop — [company]
Body
Hi [first name], Closing the loop on my last couple notes. If the timing's off, that's completely fine — I'll leave it here for now. If [specific pain] becomes more pressing down the line, you know where to find me. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: genuinely lowering the temperature — not a fake-out — is what makes people comfortable enough to finally answer honestly.

The Redirect Ask

21

Use when you suspect you may not have the right contact at the company.

Subject
wrong person?
Body
Hi [first name], Realized I might not be reaching the right person on this — if [specific pain] lives with someone else on your team, a quick point in the right direction would be a huge help. If it is you, no pressure either way — just say the word. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: this one question alone frequently gets a reply where three "just checking in" emails got silence — it's genuinely useful to answer.

The One-Question Close

22

Use when you want the absolute lowest-effort reply possible — a single word answers it.

Subject
one quick question
Body
Hi [first name], Simple one: is [specific pain] still something [company] is dealing with, or has it already been solved another way? Either answer is useful to me — just trying to know whether to keep this on your radar. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a yes/no question is nearly frictionless to answer, and either answer tells you exactly what to do next.

Follow-up 3 — Day 14, "last practical value"

One more attempt before the breakup — offer something useful with zero ask attached, which flips the relationship from "selling" to "helping."

The Free-Resource Offer

23

Use when you have something genuinely useful (a checklist, benchmark, template) unrelated to buying.

Subject
no pitch — just this
Body
Hi [first name], Not going to pitch again — figured I'd just pass along [useful free resource, e.g. "the checklist we use for X"] in case it's helpful regardless of whether we ever work together. If timing changes on your end, my inbox is always open. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: giving something with genuinely no ask attached is the single best way to be remembered positively after three unanswered emails.

The Benchmark Share

24

Use when you can share an industry number that's useful on its own, not tied to a purchase decision.

Subject
a number worth knowing, unrelated to my pitch
Body
Hi [first name], Came across this and thought of [company]: [relevant industry stat or benchmark]. Worth knowing regardless of what you decide on my end. If it's ever useful to compare notes, I'm around. [sender name] · [email]

Why it works: a genuinely useful data point positions you as informed and low-pressure right before the sequence ends.

The Honest Check-In

25

Use when you'd rather just ask directly than dress up a fourth attempt.

Subject
still worth a shot, or should I stop?
Body
Hi [first name], I've sent a few notes on [topic] without a response — totally fine if it's not a fit, just want to respect your inbox. One more email after this and I'll leave it be unless I hear otherwise. Want me to keep going, or call it here? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: naming the sequence out loud is disarming — most people respect the honesty enough to answer, even just to say stop.

4 · Breakup email

6 templates

The last email in a cold sequence, Day 21+. Counterintuitively the highest open-rate email in most sequences — "closing this out" triggers a very different psychology than "checking in" does.

The Clean Close

26

Use when the sequence is genuinely ending and you want the door left open without lingering.

Subject
closing your file — last note
Body
Hi [first name], I'll stop reaching out after this — figured silence means it's not the right time, and that's completely fine. If [specific pain] ever becomes urgent, my info's below and I'm easy to find. Wishing [company] well either way. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: announcing you're stopping removes the low-grade guilt of an open thread, which often prompts the reply that three "gentle nudges" couldn't.

The Self-Aware Breakup

27

Use when you want a lighter, slightly self-deprecating tone that still respects their time.

Subject
taking the hint
Body
Hi [first name], A few emails with no reply usually means one of two things: bad timing, or not a fit. Either way, I'll take the hint and stop here. If it's the former and things change, feel free to reach back out any time — no hard feelings. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a bit of self-awareness makes the sender feel human, which is often the missing ingredient after several polished pitches.

The Ask-for-Redirect Breakup

28

Use when you still suspect the wrong contact, and this is genuinely the last shot to find out.

Subject
last try — right person?
Body
Hi [first name], Last note from me on this — if [specific pain] isn't your area, a one-line pointer to whoever it does belong to would genuinely help, and I'll take it from there. If it is you and the timing's just off, no worries at all — I'll step back. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: combining the exit with a genuinely useful ask gets the redirect people were too busy to send earlier in the sequence.

The Value-Recap Breakup

29

Use when you want the last email to also double as a clean, quotable summary in case it gets forwarded internally.

Subject
the short version, for the record
Body
Hi [first name], Last note — in case it's useful to have in one place: [product one-liner], [proof point], [price point]. I'll stop here unless I hear otherwise. Appreciate you reading this far either way. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a clean, forwardable summary means the breakup email can still do work internally even if the direct recipient never replies.

The Timing-Flag Breakup

30

Use when you believe interest exists but budget/quarter timing is the real blocker.

Subject
flag this for [future date/quarter]?
Body
Hi [first name], Guessing this might just be bad timing on the budget/quarter side rather than a fit issue — happy to circle back around [future date, e.g. "start of next quarter"] instead of continuing to email now. Want me to flag it for then, or should I close this out for good? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: offering a specific future date instead of a vague "keep in touch" gives a busy reader a real, low-effort choice to make.

The Blunt Breakup

31

Use when the recipient's earlier emails suggest they prefer short and direct over warm and wordy.

Subject
last one — closing this out
Body
Hi [first name], No reply usually means no — closing this out on my end. If that's wrong, one line is all it takes to keep this open. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: matching the recipient's own communication style (short, direct) removes friction that a longer warm close would add for this type of reader.

5 · Re-engagement of a dead lead

6 templates

A contact who went fully cold months ago — after a breakup email or no response at all. These work because enough time has passed that a fresh, specific reason to write again feels earned, not desperate.

The New-Feature Reopen

32

Use when you've shipped something new since the last contact that directly addresses their earlier objection.

Subject
the thing that was missing last time — now live
Body
Hi [first name], It's been a while since we last talked about [specific pain] — since then we've added [new feature/change], which was the exact gap you flagged back then. Worth a fresh look now that that's solved? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: directly addressing their remembered objection proves you actually listened the first time, which most cold outreach never does.

The Quarterly Check-In

33

Use when nothing specific has changed, but enough time (60–90 days) has passed to justify a light touch.

Subject
still on my radar for [company]
Body
Hi [first name], It's been a few months since we last connected — no big update, just wanted to check whether [specific pain] is still on your plate or if it got solved another way. If it's still open, happy to pick the conversation back up wherever we left it. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: low-key persistence spread across months, not days, catches people at a completely different point in their priorities.

The Company-Change Reopen

34

Use when the prospect's company has had a real change — new funding, leadership, growth — since you last spoke.

Subject
saw [company change] — reopening this?
Body
Hi [first name], Saw [company] just [company change, e.g. "closed a Series A" / "opened a second location"] — congrats. That kind of change is usually exactly when [specific pain] starts to bite harder. Given that, worth reopening the conversation from a while back? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a real company-level trigger event is a legitimate, fresh reason — it's not "checking in," it's responding to news.

The Role-Change Reopen

35

Use when your original contact got promoted, or a new person now holds the relevant role.

Subject
congrats on the new role — reviving an old thread
Body
Hi [first name], Congrats on [new title/promotion] — saw the update. We spoke a while back about [specific pain], and figured your new seat might be exactly the right vantage point to revisit it. Worth a fresh 10 minutes given the new scope? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a promotion often means new budget authority or a wider mandate — the same pitch can land completely differently than it did before.

The New-Proof Reopen

36

Use when you've landed a much stronger result or case study since the last conversation.

Subject
the result that would've changed my last pitch
Body
Hi [first name], Since we last talked, [peer company/industry] got [strong new proof point] using the same approach I pitched you a while back. Wanted to put that in front of you in case it changes the math on your end. Worth another look? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: a stronger result than what they originally passed on gives a legitimate reason the "no" from before might not hold anymore.

The Straight Reopen

37

Use when there's no specific trigger — just enough time passed to be worth one honest attempt.

Subject
worth reopening this?
Body
Hi [first name], It's been [timeframe, e.g. "about four months"] since we last talked about [specific pain] — no news to report, just wanted to see if it's still relevant or if priorities shifted. If it's still a real problem, I'd genuinely like another shot at helping. [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: honesty about there being no news is disarming — it doesn't manufacture urgency, which makes the ask feel more trustworthy.

6 · Inbound-reply handling

6 templates

They replied — now speed and tone matter more than cleverness. These cover the most common reply types you'll actually get.

Reply to "Tell me more"

38

Use when they respond with genuine interest but no specific question yet.

Subject
re: [original subject]
Body
Hi [first name], Happy to. Short version: [2-sentence expanded pitch]. The easiest next step is usually a 15-minute call so I can see your exact setup and tell you honestly whether it's a fit — no generic demo. Does [day/time option 1] or [day/time option 2] work? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: offering two specific times instead of "let me know your availability" removes a full back-and-forth round trip.

Reply to "Not right now"

39

Use when they decline but leave the door open for later.

Subject
re: [original subject]
Body
Totally understand, [first name] — appreciate you letting me know rather than going quiet. Mind if I check back in around [specific future date]? And if there's a better time to revisit before then, just say the word. [sender name]

Why it works: proposing a specific future date (not "I'll check in sometime") gets explicit permission, which makes the eventual re-engagement email far less cold.

Reply to "Send more info"

40

Use when they ask for materials instead of agreeing to a call — a soft stall that still needs momentum.

Subject
re: [original subject]
Body
Sure thing — attached/linked below. One thing that usually saves time versus reading through it cold: a 10-minute call where I can point straight to the parts relevant to [company]'s [specific pain]. Open to that instead, or after you've had a look? [link/attachment] [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: fulfilling the request while still offering the call keeps momentum instead of letting the thread go quiet into "I'll read it later."

Reply to a Referral Request

41

Use when they reply pointing you to a colleague instead of engaging themselves.

Subject
re: [original subject]
Body
Appreciate the redirect, [first name] — I'll reach out to [referred contact] directly and mention you pointed me their way. Thanks for taking the time to reply at all — a lot of people don't. [sender name]

Why it works: a short, genuinely grateful close preserves the relationship with the original contact in case they're relevant again later.

Reply to Interest + a Question

42

Use when they ask one specific, answerable question — pricing, timeline, integration, etc.

Subject
re: [original subject]
Body
Good question — [direct, specific answer]. Happy to go deeper on that on a quick call if useful, or keep it to email if you'd rather. Either way, does this change how [company] is thinking about it? [sender name] · [phone] · [email]

Why it works: answering directly and briefly (not deflecting to "let's hop on a call") builds trust that you're not hiding behind a sales process.

Reply to "Wrong Person"

43

Use when they say it's not their area but don't volunteer a name.

Subject
re: [original subject]
Body
No problem at all, [first name] — appreciate you saying so instead of leaving me guessing. Any chance you know off the top of your head who handles [specific pain] at [company]? Even just a name is a huge help. [sender name]

Why it works: a specific, easy-to-answer question gets a name far more often than a generic "who should I talk to instead?"

7 · Pricing objection

4 templates

"Too expensive" is rarely about the number — it's about unclear value, wrong comparison, or bad timing. These reframe rather than discount by default.

The Cost-of-Inaction Reframe

44

Use when the prospect compares your price to $0 (doing nothing) instead of to what the current pain costs them.

Subject

What keeps an inbox healthy

Deliverability rules

These are the actual limits enforced by the outreach system this file is built from — not theoretical guidance. They exist because breaking them gets an inbox flagged, throttled, or blacklisted.

Daily send cap

20–25 sends per inbox per day. This is the ceiling Gmail and most providers tolerate from a single account before triggering spam-pattern detection — going over it risks the whole domain's reputation, not just that day's batch.

Jitter between sends

A random 45–120 second gap between each send. Sending 20 emails back-to-back in under a minute is the single clearest bot signature — real humans don't send that fast.

New-inbox warmup

Start at 10–15/day for the first two weeks on any new sending address, then ramp to the full daily cap. A brand-new inbox sending at full volume on day one is a near-guaranteed flag.

Suppression list

Every "no thanks," bounce, or opt-out goes on a permanent suppression list checked before every single send — reruns of a campaign must never re-contact a suppressed address.

Sent-log dedupe

Log every send with a timestamp and check it before sending again. Without this, reruns or overlapping campaigns will double-email the same contact — a fast way to get marked as spam by an annoyed human.

Personalization gate

Before anything sends, verify the body actually contains the company name or at least two distinctive details from your research. If it doesn't, it's a template blast wearing a mail-merge costume — hold it back.

Duplicate-body check

If more than two outgoing emails in a batch share a near-identical opening, that's a sign personalization broke down somewhere upstream — flag and rewrite rather than send as-is.

CAN-SPAM minimums

Real sender name, a real physical address (city/state is sufficient), a truthful subject line, and a one-line, honored opt-out mechanism — on every commercial email, no exceptions.
