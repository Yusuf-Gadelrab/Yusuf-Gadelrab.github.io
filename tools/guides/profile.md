## 8b. Nonprofit and education work

CODESWITCH — https://yusuf-gadelrab.github.io/codeswitch.html
A free bilingual introductory computer science program for high school students who are English
learners, founded by Yusuf Gadelrab out of his SIGCSE TS 2026 research on bilingual coding
environments.

The premise: every programming language is written in English — if, while, return, class — and
every error message is an English sentence, so a student still acquiring English is asked to learn
two languages at once and then graded as though they were only learning one.

The method is bilingual instruction rather than translated materials. Four rules govern every
session: concepts are introduced in the student's home language before any English keyword appears;
English keywords are treated as proper nouns to recognise rather than vocabulary to master; one real
error message is decoded aloud word by word each session; and students may ask, answer and think in
whichever language they choose, with no correction of their English during a CS explanation.

Three programs:
- The Bridge — an 8-week, 90-minute-per-week after-school intro CS cohort in Python, 8 to 12
  students, two bilingual near-peer tutors, ending with each student demoing a project they chose
  to their family in the language of their choice.
- Mirror Docs — a free, open library of bilingual CS materials: terminology mapped across languages
  with real explanations rather than dictionary glosses, and error-message decoders. Spanish and
  Vietnamese first, then Arabic.
- Near-Peer Corps — bilingual undergraduates trained as tutors. Near-peer is the pedagogy, not a
  budget compromise: someone two years ahead still remembers what was confusing.

Economics: roughly $200 per cohort and about $92 per student per year, because tutors are trained
volunteers rather than paid staff.

Evaluation: persistence (does the student enrol in a CS course afterwards), self-efficacy (a
six-item pre/post survey administered in the student's home language), and conceptual understanding
(a bilingual concept check plus a project the student can explain). Results are published whether or
not they support the hypothesis.

Safeguarding: every tutor completes district volunteer clearance, background check, TB screening
where required, and mandated-reporter training before their first session; two adults are present at
all times; there is no private contact between tutors and students.

Status as of July 2026: curriculum, volunteer handbook, partnership kit, funding plan and
evaluation design are written and public. There is not yet a signed school partner, a cleared tutor
corps, or a completed cohort, and CODESWITCH is not yet an incorporated nonprofit. First cohort is
planned for fall 2026 in San Jose, California.
