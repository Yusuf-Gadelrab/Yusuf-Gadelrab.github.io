Correction: I have one peer-reviewed SIGCSE paper

Published 2026-07-29 · Corrected 2026-08-10 · Yusuf Gadelrab

Retraction notice. The original version of this page, published 2026-07-29, announced a pair of accepted SIGCSE TS 2026 contributions. One of them was not mine. This page has been rewritten in place, at its original address, so that anything linking here lands on the correction rather than on a dead link.

I have one peer-reviewed publication. For a while I said I had more than that, and I was wrong. Here is the correction, the evidence I checked it against, and what I have changed.

What is true. I am a co-author on Exploring Bilingual Coding for Inclusive Computer Science Learning, in the Proceedings of the 57th ACM Technical Symposium on Computer Science Education (SIGCSE TS 2026), V.2 — doi:10.1145/3770761.3777339. There are eight authors: Tshukudu, Shah, Kieu, Deeb, Venkateswaran, Ghai, Gadelrab, Hada. I am seventh of eight. It is a mixed-methods, IRB-approved study of 60 participants, run in Dr. Ethel Tshukudu's CS Education Research Lab at San José State University.

The finding still stands and I still think it matters. Across the 60 participants there were statistically significant pre-to-post gains in programming confidence, computing identity, enjoyment and motivation, and novices gained significantly more than experienced programmers. The bilingual-versus-English comparison itself was not statistically significant (Cohen's d 0.25–0.40), and the paper reports no percentage figures at all — read the effects out of the paper, not out of a blog post. Confidence is not a soft metric in intro CS. It is the thing that decides whether someone comes back for the second class.

What was not true. I also listed a poster, on using knowledge graphs and retrieval to sequence CS material around what an individual learner already knows. That poster exists and it was presented at the same conference. It is not mine. It belongs to a research team at Pace University, and their four names are the ones on the registered record. Mine is not among them. I have removed it from this site, from my resume, and from the machine-readable files that search engines and AI crawlers read.

How I checked. The publisher's deposit with Crossref is the authoritative record, and for that poster it lists four authors, none of them me. A search by title returns exactly one work, so there is no alternate version carrying my name. Separately, Crossref notifies ORCID whenever a publisher attaches your ORCID iD to a submission. Mine fired three times — February, April and June 2026 — and every one of those notices says Works (1): the bilingual coding paper, and nothing else. OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and DBLP independently show the same count of one.

How it happened. I do not have a clean account of it, and I am not going to invent one. What I can say is that I never held an acceptance record for that poster, and I did not go and check before I wrote it down. I repeated something I believed instead of something I had verified. The fix for that is not a better memory. It is a source you can point at.

If this reached you. If you read the earlier version of this post, or received an application, a message, or a CV from me that mentioned a second SIGCSE contribution, this page is the correction and it supersedes them. The one citation to use is the DOI above.

I would rather have one claim I can fully stand behind than a longer list I cannot. Leaving this page up, at the address it always had, is part of that.

The rest of the original post I still stand by: I got into that lab as a freshman by emailing a professor and offering to do the boring part. Every undergrad reading this can do the same thing. The email is free. Just check what you write down afterwards.
