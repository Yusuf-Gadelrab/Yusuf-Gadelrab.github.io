Passage — the paperwork should not be the thing that stops you.

Free navigation tools, plain-language guides, and eligibility-filtered scholarship matching for immigrant, international, first-generation and undocumented students. When a question needs a lawyer, Passage refers you to one instead of guessing.

Where this actually is

Passage has served zero students. No guide has been published under the Miftah name and no referral relationships are in place. The parent organization is not incorporated, has filed nothing with the IRS and has nothing pending, so nothing given to it is deductible on anyone's return and none is being asked for. What follows is the design and the tooling it rests on.

Miftah Foundation is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice. Passage provides general information and referrals only. Immigration outcomes depend on individual facts — consult a licensed immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative before acting. Find free and low-cost help at immigrationadvocates.org/legaldirectory.

The Problem

The information exists. It just costs hundreds of dollars an hour.

A student on a dependent visa finds out at nineteen that their status has an age cliff. Another discovers in April that the scholarship they qualified for closed in January, because nobody told them citizenship was not a requirement. A third does not apply anywhere at all, because every form asks a question they are afraid to answer.

None of this is hidden. It is spread across federal regulations, campus offices, PDFs and forum threads, and the people who can assemble it into an answer charge by the hour. The students who most need it are the least able to pay for it.

What It Is

Maps of the system, and a phone number for the parts we can't map.

Plain-language guides and timeline tools

What the common student status pathways actually involve, in order, with the deadlines that matter and the ones that do not. Written for someone reading it at midnight, not for someone billing for it.

Eligibility-filtered scholarship matching

Most scholarship lists waste a student's time by showing awards they cannot receive. Passage filters on the fields that actually disqualify people — citizenship, residency, status, institution — so what's left is worth an evening.

Referral, every time it matters

Anything that depends on an individual's specific facts goes to a licensed immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative. Passage will keep a live list of free and low-cost providers and hand students to them.

Career and application navigation

Which employers sponsor, what work authorization questions on applications mean, how to read a job posting's status requirements. General information about how the process works, not advice about a specific case.

How It Works

Four moves, and one of them is always "hand off."

Orient

A student answers a few structural questions about where they are. No names, no A-numbers, no documents.

Read

They get the guides and timeline tools relevant to their situation, in plain language, free, with no account required.

Match

Scholarships are filtered against their real eligibility, so the list they get is short and actually applicable.

Refer

Anything fact-specific goes to a licensed attorney or DOJ-accredited representative, with the free and low-cost options named first.

Who It's For

Named plainly.

International and dependent-status students
People whose right to stay has a date on it, and whose plans have to be built around that date rather than around the academic calendar.
Undocumented students
Who need to know what is genuinely available to them, and who deserve information that does not require them to identify themselves to get it.
First-generation immigrant students
Whose families cannot walk them through a system nobody in the household has been through either.

The Hard Line

Where Passage stops, and why it stops there.

Immigration advice from a non-attorney is unauthorized practice of law. For immigration specifically it also carries federal penalties, and the people harmed are the students. So the boundary is drawn early and enforced everywhere, not just in a footer.

  • No advice about your specific case, ever. Passage will not tell you what to file, when to file it, or what your chances are. It will tell you how the process generally works and who is qualified to answer that question for you.
  • No form preparation. Passage does not fill out, review or submit immigration forms on anyone's behalf.
  • No document collection. Passage does not want your passport, your I-20, your A-number or your immigration file, and will not store them.
  • No representation. Nobody at Miftah Foundation appears for anyone before USCIS, an immigration court, or any agency.
  • No prediction of outcomes. Outcomes depend on individual facts. Anyone who promises you one is a warning sign, including us.
  • Volunteers are trained on this boundary before they talk to a single student, and the disclaimer goes on every guide, every page and every email, not just this one.

Honest Status

What this is, and what it is not yet.

What this is

Real today

The tooling exists and runs. A visa timeline planner and a scholarship-matching engine were both built and used — the matcher has surfaced roughly two hundred eligibility-filtered scholarship packets for a single student. The founder built this out of his own situation: he navigated the US student-immigration system first-hand, before becoming a US permanent resident.

What it is not yet

Not true yet

Passage has served zero students. No guide has been published under the Miftah name, no referral relationships are in place with legal providers, no volunteers are trained, and the parent organization is not incorporated. That packet count is a tool output for one person, not a program result.

Year-one targets — targets, not results

  • Target: published plain-language guides covering the most common student status pathways.
  • Target: formal referral relationships with at least two free or low-cost legal providers.
  • Target: students reaching a filed application or a funded scholarship — the only outcome that counts, measured at the finish line rather than at the click.

First 100 Days

What the first hundred days would actually look like.

Counted from the day one licensed referral partner signs on. That is deliberately first: publishing guides before there is somewhere to send the hard questions would be the single most dangerous version of this program.

  1. Days 1–15 · the hard gate

    Sign one referral partner and write the boundary down

    A licensed immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative who will take referrals, plus a one-page referral protocol naming exactly which question types are handed over on sight. Nothing publishes before this exists.

  2. Days 16–40

    Publish the first three guides

    The three pathways students ask about most, written in plain language, each carrying the not-a-law-firm disclaimer in the body rather than the footer, and each reviewed by the referral partner before it goes up.

  3. Days 41–55

    Ship the eligibility filter with no account required

    Filtering on the fields that actually disqualify people, running client-side, storing nothing and asking for no name. A student who is afraid to identify themselves must still be able to use it.

  4. Days 56–70

    Get one campus office to hand it to real students

    A financial-aid desk, a counsellor or a student organization willing to say "start here". Distribution through an office a student already trusts beats any amount of traffic.

  5. Days 71–90

    First cohort of real users, measured at the finish line

    The metric is applications actually filed and awards actually funded — not sessions, not clicks, not downloads. Referrals made and referrals accepted are counted separately, because only the second one means a student got help.

  6. Days 91–100

    Publish what worked and what did not

    Including guides nobody read and referrals that went nowhere. A navigation program that only reports its wins is misinforming the exact people it claims to serve.

Get Involved

What would actually help.

An immigration attorney or DOJ-accredited representative willing to take referrals. A campus office willing to point students here. Those are the two things that make Passage real.
