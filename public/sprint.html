The method

The seven passes I run on every profile

Nothing here is a secret. This is the exact order I work in, written out so you can judge the method before you pay for it — or run it yourself and keep the $97.

Pick one target role and write it down

Every later decision, which wins survive, which keywords go in, what the headline claims, gets judged against that one role. A profile aimed at three roles reads as aimed at none.

Rewrite the headline to lead with an outcome, not a job title

"Computer Science Student" is the same headline as thousands of other students. Lead with what you produce for someone, name the niche, and put a real number in it. LinkedIn allows 220 characters, and the opening words are what show up in search results and next to every comment you leave.

Rebuild the About so the first two lines survive the fold

LinkedIn hides the rest of the About section behind a see-more link, so the opening lines carry the whole thing. Open with the claim, not with "I am a Computer Science student at". Then structure the rest: what you do, proof with numbers, your edge, who you help, the offer, and one clear call to action.

Turn every experience bullet into a number first, then the action

Percent, dollars, time saved, scale, headcount, whichever you actually have. If you think you have no numbers, count something: participants, sources, hours, files, people who used a thing you built. Honest estimates labelled as estimates beat adjectives.

Map ten to fourteen keywords and place them deliberately

Take the phrasing from real job postings for your target role rather than from how you already describe yourself, then place those terms across the headline, the About, the experience entries and the skills list. Recruiter search reads all four, so a keyword that lives in only one of them is half-wasted.

Reorder skills so the first five match the target role

The top of the list is what a human scans and what recruiter filters key on, and endorsements concentrate there. Keep fifteen, order them for the role you named in step one, and treat everything past that as decoration.

Write the banner brief last, once the words are settled

A banner is 1584 x 396 pixels. It should repeat the one claim from your headline in six words or fewer, in a single accent colour, with one proof element. Doing it last stops you designing around a message you are about to change.
