01 · Why

King, sword, gold — in one figure.

Two brands, two languages, one silhouette that carries both.

My clothing label is KXNG SEF: king, plus سيف — Arabic for sword, and the nickname I have had since childhood. My studio is DHAHAB: ذهب, gold. A lion's mane is the one figure that carries both without needing a word in either language. It also survives being shrunk: at 16 pixels a monogram turns to mush, but a mane silhouette is still a mane.

02 · Construction

Three decisions do all the work.

Everything is generated from one Python file. These are the parts that took the most iterations to get right — and the failure mode each one prevents.

The mane hangs

Shard length is a function of angle — cropped over the brow, heaviest at the jaw, with two low harmonics for an organic outline. A constant radius does not read as a lion. It reads as a sun.

The shards vary

Lengths carry a deterministic jitter. Identical petals at even spacing read as a flower, not a mane — same reason a hand-drawn mane is never symmetrical.

The muzzle falls

A philtrum line under the nose and lobes that drop away. Lift those lobe ends by a few units and the whole mark turns into a smiley face. This is the single most fragile detail in the design.

03 · Colour

Gold on black, or ink on bone.

No third colourway. The gradient runs light to dark, top to bottom, and never rotates.

Ink #0A0A0B The mark's own ground. Not a text colour — the page ground it sits on is #0B0B0D.
Gold #D4AF37 --gold-400 · measured 8.08:1 on the dark surfaces. Safe as text.
Gradient F4E2A1→A9821F Fill only. It exists inside the mark's geometry and is never set on type.
Bone #F2EDE4 --ink · measured 14.57:1 on the dark surfaces. The primary copy colour.

The ramp behind it

One hue family, eight stops, so no page ever has to invent a gold. Ratios are the measured worst case across the four dark surfaces the site uses.

--gold-100#F7E9B814.00:1 — brightest legible stop
--gold-200#EFD98F12.13:1
--gold-300#E9C96A10.54:1 — gold body text on a dark surface
--gold-400#D4AF378.08:1 — the brand gold
--gold-500#B8942F5.92:1
--gold-600#9E832E4.64:1 — the AA floor, and the last stop that may hold text
--gold-700#836D263.38:1 on dark — paper only (4.81:1 on #FBFAF7)
--gold-800#5C4C1B2.03:1 — decorative only. Rules and fills, never ink.

Every ratio above was measured with the WCAG 2.x formula against the four dark surfaces the site actually paints, and the worst of the four is the one quoted. Two stops are disqualified from ever holding text: --gold-700 is paper-only and --gold-800 is decorative. A gold that looks right and fails a reader is not on brand.

04 · Type

Three faces, none of them downloaded.

The site makes zero external font requests, on any page, ever. Everything below is a face your machine already had.

The honest cost of that rule: I do not control which face you are reading. The serif above resolves differently on macOS, Windows and Linux, and on an unlucky machine it lands on Times New Roman. That is the trade — a page that renders instantly and phones nobody, in exchange for a specimen I cannot guarantee. I take it every time.

08 · Rules

Five, and they are not negotiable.

Written down because a rule you keep in your head is a rule you break at 2am.

  • Two colourways only. Gold on black, or ink on bone. Never a third.
  • Clear space of one ear-width — about 12% of the mark — on every side.
  • 24px minimum for the master mark, 16px for the tile. Below that, use the tile.
  • Never recolour individual shards, add an outline, stretch it unevenly, or drop it on a busy photo.
  • On garments the lion takes the gold only when it is the largest graphic with no competing hero word. Beside a giant word, it prints bone like any other supporting element — the same one-hero-element rule the crown follows, with no carve-out.
