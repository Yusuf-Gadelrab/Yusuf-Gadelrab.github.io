Two papers accepted to SIGCSE 2026. I'm an undergrad

2026-07-29 · Yusuf Gadelrab

The first one asks a question that sounds simple and isn't: what happens to a student learning to code when the programming language is in English and their thinking isn't?

We ran a mixed-methods study, 60 participants, IRB approved, in Dr. Ethel Tshukudu's CSEd lab at SJSU. Across the 60 participants there were statistically significant pre-to-post gains in programming confidence, computing identity, enjoyment and motivation, with novices gaining significantly more than experienced programmers. The bilingual-versus-English comparison itself was not statistically significant (Cohen's d 0.25–0.40), and the paper reports no percentage figures — read the effects out of the paper, not out of this post.

Confidence is not a soft metric in intro CS. Confidence is the thing that decides whether someone comes back for the second class. Retention in CS1 is a leaky bucket and most of the leak happens in the first six weeks, before anyone has learned enough to fail on the merits.

The second paper is a poster on adaptive curriculum maps: graph-augmented retrieval over course material, so an LLM tutor knows what the student hasn't learned yet instead of confidently explaining recursion to someone who hasn't seen a function.

Paper 1: https://doi.org/10.1145/3770761.3777339
