01 Miftah Foundation · Program 01 · in design

CodeBridge — code in your own language.

Free bilingual computer science — Spanish and Arabic alongside English — designed for partner high schools and to be released openly online. For students who already think fluently in one language and are being asked to learn two hard things at once.

Where this actually is

No cohort has run. No school has signed anything and no volunteer has been trained. The parent organization is not incorporated, has filed nothing with the IRS and has nothing pending, so nothing given to it is deductible on anyone's return and none is being asked for. What follows is the design and the research it rests on.

The Problem

The barrier is the language, not the logic.

A student who reasons fluently in Spanish or Arabic sits down to an English-only curriculum, with English-only error messages, English-only documentation and an English-speaking teacher. They are debugging their English at the same time as their code — and when they struggle, everyone in the room reads it as "not a coder."

The logic was never the hard part. The translation tax was. San José has large Spanish-speaking and Arabic-speaking student communities, and very little CS instruction that meets them in their own language.

What It Is

A CS curriculum that speaks both languages at once.

Bilingual by design, not translated after

Concepts arrive in the learner's stronger language, and the English technical vocabulary is taught alongside them, deliberately, as its own skill. Students finish able to read English documentation — that is the actual job — without having had to guess their way to the concept underneath it.

Free, in person, and openly published

The delivery model is one cohort at a partner high school, taught by trained volunteers. Every lesson, exercise and slide would be released under an open licence, so any teacher anywhere can run it without asking permission or paying anybody.

Statistically significant pre-to-post gains in programming confidence, computing identity, enjoyment and motivation across 60 participants — with novices gaining significantly more than experienced programmers.

Exploring Bilingual Coding for Inclusive Computer Science Learning, SIGCSE TS 2026 · doi:10.1145/3770761.3777339
Read that carefully. It is a result from a university study the founder co-authored — seventh of eight authors — not a CodeBridge outcome. CodeBridge has never run a cohort and has no results of its own.

How It Works

Four moves, one cohort.

Partner

A high school or community college agrees to host a cohort and provides the room, the schedule and the students.

Baseline

Students take a short pre-survey on confidence and prior exposure. The same instrument runs again at the end, so the delta is honest.

Teach

Weekly sessions across a full term, taught bilingually by a trained volunteer, with the open curriculum available to students at home.

Hand off

Graduates route to Passage for scholarship and application navigation, and are invited back as mentors in the language they learned in.

Who It's For

Named plainly.

High-school students
Grades 9 to 12 at partner schools in San José, especially campuses with no AP CS offering at all.
First-year college students
Anyone taking their first programming course who is quietly drowning in the vocabulary rather than the material.
Spanish and Arabic speakers first
Those two languages come first because that is where the instructional design and the volunteer bench would actually exist. Other languages follow real capacity, not ambition.

The Limits

What CodeBridge will not do.

  • No program touches minors until the safeguarding policy exists. Parental consent forms, a background-check policy for adult volunteers, a two-adult rule, a mandated-reporter acknowledgment. Until those are written and adopted, there is no cohort. This gate is not negotiable and it will not be quietly dropped.
  • Not a bootcamp and not a job guarantee. A term of CS instruction is a beginning. Anyone promising employment outcomes from a free high-school cohort is selling something.
  • Not a replacement for a school's CS department. CodeBridge runs alongside partner schools, with their teachers, on their terms.
  • Not machine translation. Bilingual instruction means a bilingual human in the room. Running the slides through a translator is not the program.
  • No student data sold, shared or published. Pre and post surveys are anonymised and reported only in aggregate.

Honest Status

What this is, and what it is not yet.

What this is

Real today

The research is published and peer-reviewed. The founder already runs CS programming at Yerba Buena High School through the SJSU CSEd lab, with expansion to Lynbrook underway. The instructional approach was tested with 60 participants under an IRB protocol.

What it is not yet

Not true yet

Zero CodeBridge cohorts have run. No curriculum has been published under the Miftah name, no volunteers are trained, no school has signed a partnership, and the parent organization is not incorporated. Every number below is a first-year target, not a result.

Year-one targets — targets, not results

  • Two partner schools, one cohort each.
  • Students completing a full cohort, measured as completion rate rather than enrolment.
  • A pre-to-post confidence delta measured with the same instrument used in the published study.
  • The full curriculum released openly, in both language pairs.

First 100 Days

What the first hundred days would actually look like.

Counted from the day a partner school says yes — not from today. Nothing in this sequence can start earlier, and the safeguarding gate at step two stops the whole thing if it is not cleared.

  1. Days 1–10

    Lock one school, one room, one term

    Named teacher of record, a fixed weekly slot, the exact cohort size, and a written understanding of who is responsible for what. A vague "we'd love to host something" is not a partner site; a calendar entry is.

  2. Days 11–25 · the hard gate

    Adopt the safeguarding policy and clear every volunteer

    Parental consent forms, background checks for adult volunteers, a two-adult rule, a mandated-reporter acknowledgment, and one named person responsible for it. If this is not finished by day 25, the cohort moves rather than starting uncovered.

  3. Days 26–45

    Build unit one in both language pairs

    Spanish–English and Arabic–English, written by bilingual instructors rather than translated afterwards, with the English technical vocabulary broken out as its own explicit track. One unit finished properly beats a whole term sketched.

  4. Days 46–50

    Run the baseline survey

    The same confidence and identity instrument used in the published study, anonymised, before a single lesson. Without a real baseline the end-of-term number is decoration.

  5. Days 51–90

    Teach the cohort

    Weekly sessions with a trained bilingual volunteer, the open curriculum available at home, and attendance tracked honestly — completion rate, not enrolment, is the number that gets reported.

  6. Days 91–100

    Measure, publish, and hand off

    Post-survey with the same instrument, curriculum released under an open licence in both language pairs, the result published on this site whether the delta is good or flat, and graduates routed to Passage and invited back as mentors.

Get Involved

What would actually help.

A bilingual volunteer who can teach, or a school that will host a cohort. Those two things unblock the program. Money does not, yet.
