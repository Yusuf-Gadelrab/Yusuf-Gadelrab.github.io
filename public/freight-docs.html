What each document is for

Rate confirmation

The rate confirmation is the contract for one specific load. It fixes the rate, the stops, the equipment and the paperwork the carrier owes you before you pay. Most payment disputes trace back to something that was never written on this page: detention terms, accessorial caps, or which documents are required for an invoice to be considered complete.

Bill of lading

The bill of lading is the receipt for the freight and the document a claim is built on. It records what was picked up, in what condition, and who took custody. The signature at delivery is what converts a dispute about damage into a documented fact, which is why a clean signed copy matters more than any email in the thread.

Carrier packet

The packet is the onboarding file you keep before a carrier ever touches your freight. Insurance certificates, authority, W-9 and a signed agreement are the minimum. If a carrier is reluctant to return any of it, that reluctance is information.

Load tender

The tender is the offer. Keeping it short and complete is what gets a callback: lane, equipment, dates, rate and a deadline. Everything else can wait for the confirmation.
