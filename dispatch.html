DHAHAB Dispatch · free weekly

Most build-in-public writing shows you the win. This one shows the arithmetic.

A weekly letter about building leverage without a budget: AI agent workflows, automation that survives contact with reality, and the honest economics underneath it. Written by a full-time CS and applied math student who pays his own API bill, which is exactly why the numbers in it are real.

Free forever. One email a week. Unsubscribe in one click.

The rules

What I will and will not send you

  • One email a week, and only if it earns the send. A week with nothing worth saying gets no email. Filler is worse than silence.
  • Real numbers or no numbers. Every figure comes with its method and its window. If something is a backtest, it says so. If it lost money, that gets written down too.
  • Nothing gets sold to you mid-letter. At most one link at the bottom, and usually it is to something free.
  • No trade alerts, no signals, no price targets. Trading shows up as a case study in testing ideas honestly, not as advice. Nothing here is financial advice.
  • Your address stays put. Never rented, never sold, never shared. One-click unsubscribe on every issue.

Already written

The first eight issues

These are finished, not planned. Subscribe now and you get them in order, one a week, starting with the first one.

Issue 01

The mistake that fixed my AI budget

Output tokens cost more than input tokens on every tier, and a chatty model answering a yes or no question is the most expensive habit you can have without noticing. What I changed, and what it actually saved.

Issue 02

Stop doing it inline. Fan it out.

One agent grinding a checklist is slower than five agents working five independent problems. How I split work into parallel lanes, and the two kinds of task that must never be parallelised.

Issue 03

I tested six trading setups. One survived.

Walk-forward testing on my own data killed five of six ideas, including two that looked excellent in-sample. The one that held up returned a modest positive expectancy in simulation, and I explain exactly why that is still not a promise.

Issue 04

How one person ships a 25-page site

Not by working 25 times harder. By building the parts that repeat once and then letting them repeat. A tour of the system, including the parts that were a waste of time.

Issue 05

My local model does the boring eighty percent

A small model on my own machine drafts. A bigger one only judges. Where that split saves real money, and the class of work where sending it local quietly costs more than it saves.

Issue 06

A research paper became a CS program

My co-authored SIGCSE paper on bilingual coding instruction did not have to stop at publication. How the finding turned into a curriculum for English-learner high schoolers, and what that took.

Issue 07

The gate that stops automation from embarrassing you

Automated writing is fast. Unchecked automated writing is a liability with your name on it. The quality gate I run before anything generated is allowed out the door.

Issue 08

What I actually earned this year

The real accounting, not the highlight reel. What made money, what made nothing, and which of my own projects I would not build again.

Who writes it

Why this letter and not another one

I am a computer science and applied math undergraduate at San José State, co-author of an ACM SIGCSE TS 2026 poster abstract in CS education, and an undergraduate researcher in the CSEd lab. I also build the things I write about: a security scanner, a set of offline apps, an outreach automation rail, and a swing screener whose results I publish whether or not they flatter me. The Dispatch is the working notebook, cleaned up once a week. It is free because the audience is the point.

FAQ

Common questions

How often does the Dispatch go out?

Once a week. If there is nothing worth sending in a given week, nothing gets sent. Filler is worse than silence.

Is it free?

Yes, and every issue currently planned is free. If a paid tier is ever added, everything already published stays free and nothing you already receive gets moved behind a paywall.

Will you sell my email address?

No. The list is not rented, sold, or shared with anyone. Every issue has a one-click unsubscribe link and unsubscribing takes effect immediately.

Is this a trading newsletter?

No. Trading comes up as one subject among several, and only as a case study in testing ideas honestly. The Dispatch does not publish trade alerts, signals, or price targets, and nothing in it is financial advice.
