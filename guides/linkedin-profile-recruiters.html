Write the profile for how recruiters actually search

A recruiter runs a keyword search, scans a list of one-line results, opens a few, and reads two lines of each before deciding. Optimise for that sequence and nothing else.

How recruiters actually find people

The workflow is search, scan, open, skim. They search on concrete terms — a language, a framework, a title, a location, sometimes a school. The results list shows your name, headline, and location, and nothing else. That headline is doing all the work of getting you opened.

So a headline of "Aspiring Software Engineer | Passionate about technology" fails twice: it contains no term anyone searches for, and it says nothing that would make someone click.

The headline

[what you are] · [proof or specialisation] | [concrete stack] | [what you are building]

Concrete beats aspirational. Load it with the nouns a recruiter would type — the language, the framework, the domain, the credential — while still reading like a human wrote it. You have around 220 characters; use most of them.

  • Include your actual stack in the words the industry uses.
  • Include one credential or proof point if you have one: a publication, a shipped product, a named employer.
  • Include what you are looking for if you are looking. Recruiters filter on "open to" language.
  • Avoid: passionate, aspiring, enthusiast, ninja, guru. They occupy space and signal nothing.

The About section: only the first two lines are read

LinkedIn truncates About behind a "see more" link. Whatever is above that fold is the entire section for most readers, so the first sentence must be the summary of you, written in plain declarative form.

The structure that works below the fold: what you do now, one or two specific pieces of proof with numbers, your stack, and what you want next, ending with contact details. Short paragraphs. No walls of text — the mobile app renders them as a grey slab nobody finishes.

Experience entries are not job descriptions

Copying your duties from the job posting is the most common waste of space on LinkedIn. Write what changed because you were there. Action, object, method, result — with the result quantified when it honestly can be.

Instead of: Responsible for maintaining internal reporting tools Write: Rebuilt the internal reporting pipeline over 50+ daily sources, cutting manual research time 60%

The mechanical points that quietly matter

  • Custom URL. linkedin.com/in/yourname, not the string of digits. It goes on the resume.
  • Photo and banner. A clear face and a banner that is not the default. This is a two-minute fix with outsized effect on reply rates.
  • Skills section. It feeds the search index. Populate it with the terms you want to be found for.
  • Featured section. Pin the portfolio, the paper, the repository. It is the only place a link gets real visual weight.
  • Open-to-work settings. The recruiter-only version is visible in their tooling without the green banner.
  • Location. Set it to where you can actually work. Recruiters filter hard on it.

Activity, briefly

You do not need to post daily. But a profile with zero activity looks abandoned, and a profile with a handful of substantive posts about what you built reads as evidence. Posting the thing you made, with a link and what you learned, is the highest-value-per-minute activity on the platform — and it is the same content as your portfolio, so it costs you nothing new.

FAQ

Quick answers

How do recruiters search?

Keyword search, then a results list showing only name, headline, and location. The headline decides whether you get opened.

What goes in the headline?

What you are, a proof point, your real stack in industry terms, and what you are building. Use most of the ~220 characters.

How long should About be?

Only the first two lines are read before the fold. Lead with a plain declarative summary, then short paragraphs with numbers.

What about Experience?

Not job duties — what changed because you were there, quantified wherever an honest number exists.

Do I need to post?

Not daily. Zero activity looks abandoned; a few substantive build posts read as evidence and reuse your portfolio work.

Which settings matter?

Custom URL, real photo and banner, populated Skills, a Featured section with your links, open-to-work for recruiters, correct location.
